OVERVIEW
Our product is a dashboard connected to Gmail and Google Calendar that turns a student's recruiting inbox into one list of applications and the actions each one needs today.
Problem: Students in active recruiting apply to dozens or hundreds of roles. Each application spawns emails like confirmations, OAs, interview scheduling, rejections, offers, and coffee chat invites. These are scattered across Gmail and Google Calendar, which are poor at showing status or deadlines across many companies, so students miss OA deadlines, forget to reply to scheduling emails, and lose track of who they talked to.


VALUE PROPOSITION
For students juggling dozens to hundreds of applications, our product turns a messy inbox into one dashboard that says exactly what needs attention today, with no system to build yourself.
Actionability: a Today view of deadlines and replies needed, ranked by urgency, instead of a static record of statuses.
Centralization: every application, email, interview, and coffee chat for a company in one place, populated automatically from Gmail.
Lower effort needed: the dashboard does the organizing, so staying on top of recruiting no longer depends on manual entry and switching between many tabs and websites. 


TARGET USER PERSONA (SEGMENT FURTHER)
Employment Fein - Sophomore or upperclassmen recruiting for finance, consulting, tech, etc. who has applied to hundreds of jobs but doesn’t have much of a system, maybe just a spreadsheet. They have a bunch of OAs to do, have quite a few coffee chats to set up, but the sheer volume is just making it hard to keep up. The automatic sync with Gmail and today’s to-do list is a game changer for them
Academic Weapon - Someone recruiting who may be a little more organized or who hasn’t applied to quite as many jobs who has a bit of a system going for them but they already don’t have a lot of time and manually re-updating their system is tedious. Our CSV import is important for them
Newbies - Freshman who might just be starting to recruit so barely knows what a good system looks like but wants to start off strong. The centralized dashboard for everything they need is a great value proposition for them.

HOW IT WORKS
Users sign in with Google once. The app backfills their recent inbox, then keeps the dashboard current as new emails arrive.

Ingestion
• Sign-in: Google sign-in through Supabase with read-only Gmail and Calendar scopes, so signing in and connecting both is one step.
Backfill: on first connect, scan the last 90 days (adjustable) so the dashboard is already full on first open if they’ve been recruiting.
Pre-filter: a Gmail search pulls only likely recruiting emails before any LLM call, for example ATS senders (greenhouse.io, lever.co, myworkday.com) and subjects like "thank you for applying," "assessment," or "interview."
Ongoing sync: check for new emails on a schedule.
CSV import and manual entry: map columns from an existing spreadsheet, and add or edit anything email misses.
Classification
An LLM labels each filtered email and extracts structured fields.

Company matching: a lot of emails come from ATS no-reply addresses, so company and role are read from the email body, then matched to existing applications to avoid duplicates.
• Fixing mistakes: there is no review queue. Results go straight to the dashboard, and users correct or delete anything in edit mode. Edits stick through later syncs.

Dashboard
Today's view: what actions need to be done, sorted by due date: OAs to complete, scheduling replies, offer deadlines, thank-you notes, and coffee-chat follow-ups.
Pipeline view: a table with one row per application. Users add, hide, and reorder columns such as status, date applied, last activity, next action, and resume.
Company page: a timeline of every email, interview, and touchpoint for that company, plus the resume used.
Touchpoints: coffee chats and recruiter calls live under the company or application.They are detected from email or added manually. 
A company can also be tracked before its application opens: it shows in the Pipeline with status Networking, its touchpoints carry over once you apply, and when the role opens the Today view can prompt "Ask [contact] for a referral."
Resume versions: manual uploads to a resume library. A version can be attached to an application; otherwise the user's default resume applies (if available). This covers tailored resumes and keeps a record after the resume changes.






VISUAL
Today: an urgency-ranked action list showing the item, company, and due date, with one-tap Done, Snooze, and Open email. Header counts show overdue, due today, and due this week.
Pipeline: the customizable table with status dropdowns and filters. A kanban view by status is a stretch.
Company page: a header (company, role, status, resume) over a vertical timeline of emails, interviews, and touchpoints.
Edit mode: a toggle on the Pipeline and Company page that makes every field editable inline, with delete, merge, and undo


Also needed: onboarding (Sign in with Google, backfill progress, first dashboard).

Privacy and Data Controls
Store extracted fields and the Gmail thread ID only. Never store email bodies, event descriptions, or calendar events outside the filter, and never log LLM inputs
The database has no email body column; logs contain no email text
Send only pre-filtered emails and event metadata (title, time, organizer, attendee domains) to the LLM, trimmed of quoted reply chains, through an API setting that doesn't retain or train on inputs
A Delete my data button removes every record, resume, and stored Google token, and revokes Gmail and Calendar access
Export all applications, events, and touchpoints to CSV
Export opens cleanly in Google Sheets
Never extract compensation, offer terms, or interviewer feedback. For offers, only the response deadline
Supabase row-level security on every table, and Google tokens encrypted at rest

ASSUMPTIONS
For our personas, managing applications after submitting is a pretty big pain
Enough of recruiting happens over email that Gmail captures most of it. Texts, Handshake in-app messages, and portal-only updates might be missed.
An LLM can classify recruiting emails accurately enough (target 90% or better) that the review queue stays short.
Students will grant read-only inbox access when data handling is explained clearly.
A Today view changes behavior: users act on deadlines sooner than they do with Gmail alone.
Resume tracking matters mainly to students who tailor resumes, so manual upload is enough.

RISKS
Setup friction
Students hesitate to grant inbox access
Duplicate or mismatched applications from ATS no-reply senders
Calendar matching pulls in personal events or misses interviews from companies not yet tracked

Data model
• Company has zero or more Applications.
• Application: role, status, date applied, resume version, source (email, CSV, or manual), and which fields the user has edited, so sync leaves them alone.
• Event: an item from an email or calendar event on an application, with category, date, extracted fields, and a link to the Gmail thread or calendar event. Email bodies and event descriptions are not stored.
• Action: a to-do with a due date, and done or snoozed state, linked to an application.
• Touchpoint: a coffee chat or call with person, date, and notes, linked to a company (even one with no application yet) or to an application.
• ResumeVersion: file, label, and upload date.
• IgnoredItem: a Gmail thread or calendar event ID the user deleted or marked Not recruiting, so sync skips it.
